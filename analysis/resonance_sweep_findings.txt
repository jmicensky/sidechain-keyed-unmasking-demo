RESONANCE MODE PARAMETER SWEEP -- FINDINGS
Construction Scene, Dialogue priority class, Resonance duck mode only
100 points, 7D Latin Hypercube: threshold -60 to -18dB, ratio 1.5-10:1,
knee 0-24dB, attack 1-50ms, release 20-400ms (same ranges as Basic/
Advanced's sweeps), num_peaks 1-6, bandwidth_octaves 0.05-4.0
Data: analysis/resonance_sweep_results_construction_dialogue.csv
Script: analysis/run_resonance_sweep.py --n 100
======================================================================

MOTIVATION: a hypothesis that Resonance mode, reconfigured as 1-2 WIDE-
bandwidth peaks rather than its current default framing (several narrow,
precise notches), might work more like a moving-band multiband compressor
- tracking wherever the key's energy currently is - and could be a viable
alternative to Basic/Advanced. Went in expecting this probably wouldn't
match Basic/Advanced's intelligibility improvement, but worth testing
rather than assuming.

METHODOLOGY NOTE - REAL BUG FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE THESE RESULTS: the
first run of this sweep produced nonsensical numbers (margin gain up to
+12.5dB - higher than anything Basic/Advanced ever produced - while STOI
gain and loudness reduction were uniformly, severely NEGATIVE across
nearly the entire parameter space, barely varying with settings at all).
Root cause: Resonance mode has a fixed, deterministic algorithmic latency
of exactly kFftSize=2048 samples (42.7ms @ 48kHz - ResonanceSuppressor.h's
kLatencySamples). Neither compute_stoi()'s lag search (+/-20ms) nor
compute_masking_margin() (no lag correction at all) accounted for this,
so every metric was comparing temporally-misaligned signals. Confirmed
directly via a wide (+/-100ms) cross-correlation search independently
landing on exactly 2048 samples. Fixed by trimming the known latency from
the rendered mix before scoring (run_resonance_sweep.py's
evaluate_condition()) - this run reflects that fix.

FINDING 1 -- Confirms the going-in hypothesis: Resonance mode's ceiling is
real but meaningfully lower than Basic/Advanced's.
  Best achieved here: margin gain +4.05dB, STOI gain +0.070, loudness
  reduction +11.5pp.
  Construction's Basic/Advanced sweep (150pt, same scene/key): margin gain
  +6.96dB, STOI gain +0.104-0.121, loudness reduction +11.4pp.
  Margin gain ceiling: ~42% lower. STOI gain ceiling: ~33-42% lower.
  Loudness reduction ceiling: essentially tied (+11.5 vs +11.4).
  Resonance mode duck the masker enough to meaningfully reduce perceived
  loudness/masking overlap, but doesn't translate that into as much raw
  level-margin or predicted-intelligibility gain as ducking a fixed,
  wider band does.

FINDING 2 -- "Wide bandwidth" (wide Q) is a real, validated lever; "few
peaks" specifically is not.
  Correlation with STOI gain:
    threshold            r = -0.746  (dominant, as in every other sweep)
    bandwidth_octaves    r = +0.416  (second-strongest - wider is better)
    release              r = +0.217
    ratio                r = +0.076
    num_peaks            r = -0.057  (essentially no relationship)
    knee                 r = -0.055
    attack               r = +0.002
  Standardized multiple regression on STOI gain (controls for all 7
  parameters simultaneously, R^2=0.813):
    threshold   -0.775
    bandwidth   +0.505   <- second-strongest independent predictor
    release     +0.112
    attack      -0.105
    ratio       +0.047
    num_peaks   +0.061   <- small, and if anything slightly POSITIVE
    knee        -0.017
  Direct comparison, peaks<=2 vs. peaks>2 (not controlling for anything
  else, just the raw sample split):
    peaks<=2 (n=30): mean STOI gain +0.0177, mean margin gain +0.60dB
    peaks>2  (n=70): mean STOI gain +0.0172, mean margin gain +0.74dB
  These are statistically indistinguishable. The top 15 points by STOI
  gain span num_peaks 1 through 5 fairly evenly (not clustered at 1-2) -
  what they share is uniformly high bandwidth (mean 2.82 octaves among the
  top 20, vs. 2.03 octaves overall).

CONCLUSION ON THE ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS: half right. Wide bandwidth is
genuinely the active ingredient - confirmed both by simple correlation and
by a regression that controls for every other parameter at once. Peak
COUNT is not - 1-2 peaks perform about the same as 3-6, once bandwidth is
already wide. So "moving-band multiband compressor" framing is directionally
right (wide, few notches DOES look more like a moving fixed-band duck than
Resonance's original narrow-notch default), but the mechanism is "duck a
wide swath wherever the key's energy is" rather than specifically "use as
few bands as possible" - num_peaks turned out to be close to a free
parameter in this search.

Even at its best (wide bandwidth, near-maximal threshold), Resonance
mode's ceiling stays below Basic/Advanced's on the two metrics that matter
most for the paper's intelligibility argument (margin gain, STOI gain) -
consistent with the user's own expectation going in. Loudness reduction
being competitive is worth noting but is the weaker of the three metrics
for judging intelligibility specifically.

NEXT STEPS NOT YET DONE:

    constant (Engine.h's kResonanceLevelCompensationDb=12.0, unchanged) -
    not verified whether that constant is still well-calibrated at the
    wide-bandwidth, few-peak configurations this sweep found best (it was
    tuned empirically for the original several-narrow-peaks framing).
  - Not tested against Public Transit or Restaurant scenes, or non-
    Dialogue priority classes.
  - The resonance-max-reduction ceiling (kept at Engine.h's own default,
    24dB, not swept) could itself be a limiting factor at the high-
    bandwidth end - not investigated separately from the swept parameters.
